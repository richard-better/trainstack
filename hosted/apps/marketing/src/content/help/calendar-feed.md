The calendar feed puts your planned workouts, and if you like what you did, into the calendar app
you already use. Your calendar app reads it from a private address; TrainStack gets no access to
your calendar.

## Turning it on

In **Settings**, under **Calendar feed**, choose **What the feed shows**:

- **Planned workouts**: workouts, notes and rest days you planned, each on its day.
- **Planned workouts and activities**: also what you did, at the time you did it, with its
  distance and load.

Then press **Turn on the calendar feed**, and add it with **Add to Apple Calendar**,
**Add to Google Calendar** or **Add to Outlook.com**, or copy the address into any calendar app
that subscribes to calendars by URL.

Calendar apps fetch the feed on their own schedule, so a change can take a while to show. Google
Calendar can take up to a day.

## Who can see it

Anyone with the address can see, without signing in:

- Your planned workouts from 4 weeks ago to 26 weeks ahead: each one's day, sport, name, planned
  time, distance and load, its steps, and your notes on it.
- Notes and rest days you put on your calendar, with their text.
- Whether you did, skipped or missed each workout, but not why you skipped it.
- With activities on, your activities of the last 4 weeks: sport, name as you wrote it, when you
  started, how long, distance and load.

It never shows your routes or where you were, or the heart rate, power or other readings your
activities recorded. Services such as Google keep a copy on their servers.

Keep the address private like a password. If it gets out, press **Make a new address**: the old
one stops working at once, and you add the new one to your calendar again. **Turn off the
calendar feed** stops it for good.

## Not supported yet

TrainStack only publishes a feed. It cannot read your calendar to find time to train, or write
real events into it.
