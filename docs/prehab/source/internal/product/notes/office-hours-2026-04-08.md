## Problem Statement

Recreational athletes (runners, climbers, cyclists, lifters) have predictable sport-specific injury patterns. The current solution — in-person physical therapy — is expensive ($100-200/session) and produces home exercise programs that most people don't follow. The failure isn't lack of a program; it's execution: exercises come home as paper lists or PDFs, users forget how to do them, there's no video reference, and the cognitive overhead of getting started kills adherence (non-adherence rate: 50-73% by research).

The product: a zero-friction guided session runner that uses AI to generate sport-specific prehab programs and delivers them as sequential, autoplay sessions — open the app, the session runs itself, done in 10-15 minutes. No hunting, no decisions, no paper notes.

Strategic context: This is the beachhead product for **Train Stack** — a fitness OS targeting the multi-sport recreational athlete. The sport profile, training volume, pain history, and session data collected here are the raw inputs for Train Stack's "What should I train today?" recommendation engine. These apps share user identity, data infrastructure, and target user.

**Category fit:** Multiple friends are paying $100-200/session for in-person PT — recurring spend. Market research confirms 50-73% non-adherence to home programs. Adapted.app has a paying user base. Market is real and large.

**The gap nobody fills:** Sport-specific AI-generated prehab with clinical credibility, fair transparent pricing, and form feedback — available to individuals without employer gating.

**Adapted is the primary direct competitor.** Launched Feb 2026 with the closest product concept. Current weaknesses: tiny exercise library (43 exercises), no sport-specific protocols, no upper body/wrist/neck coverage. They're 6 weeks old with 17 ratings — **the window to out-execute them is now.** Adapted also uses real LLM generation (not rule-based like GoWod/Pliability/StretchIt) — their AI is real but their content depth is the gap.

**Pricing:** Adapted's $8.99/mo ($39.99/yr) has claimed the "affordable" position. The middle ($50-80/yr) is dangerous — no clear positioning against either end. Go clearly premium: **$12/mo ($120/yr)**. This signals clinical quality over Adapted while undercutting Pliability's billing-tainted $180/yr. At $12/mo, ~1,850 subscribers = $200K profit.

**Trust moat:** Transparent billing is a real differentiator — Pliability and StretchIt both have it as their #1 negative review theme. Lead with "cancel anytime, no hidden fees" in marketing copy and onboarding.

**Capacity/trust scale:** Adopt Adapted's 0-4 "how much do you trust this region of your body" framing instead of a pain scale. More clinically accurate for prehab (capability, not suffering) and already validated by the only direct competitor. Replace "pain areas" intake with capacity rating per body region.

**Progress measurement gap (High priority, underserved by all competitors):** Nobody closes the loop — measure → program → remeasure → show delta. For prehab, "not getting hurt" is hard to celebrate. The retention hook is concrete sport metrics: "ran 5K pain-free for the first time in 3 months" or "back climbing at your project grade." This is the feature that converts users into advocates. Build from day one alongside session tracking.

**What competitors' "AI" actually means:**

- GoWod, StretchIt, Pliability: questionnaire → preset program lookup. Not AI.
- Adapted: real LLM generation with feedback loop. Direct competition.
- Hinge Health: CV/ML for real-time form correction. Different type, B2B only.

The LLM generation gap is real but closing fast. Sport-specific depth + progress measurement is the durable moat.

## Status Quo

People in the PT ecosystem take one of three paths home:

1. **Paper lists / PDFs** — exercises with text descriptions, no video, easy to lose, high cognitive overhead
2. **Random YouTube searches** — friction that kills momentum before the session starts
3. **PT videotaping the patient** — effective (the PT literally solved the problem) but not scalable

What's common: by the time someone would do their home exercises, the decision cost — what to do, in what order, how exactly — is high enough that most people skip. Quote: "It's already hard to get started; it's very hard to actually keep doing these exercises."

The competitor ([P]rehab) solves the library problem but has a different failure mode: too many exercises, too much browsing, not enough specificity (no shoulder-specific exercises for this user's needs). It's a better reference tool, not a guided session runner.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** The recreational multi-sport athlete already doing some form of PT or prehab awareness — runner, climber, cyclist, lifter — who wants to prevent predictable sport-specific injuries before they become serious. Not someone who can't access PT; someone who has a program (or needs one) but fails at home execution.

**Why they specifically:** They know they should be doing this. They've been to PT. The barrier isn't knowledge or willingness — it's friction. They're the highest-intent, lowest-barrier segment.

**Narrowest wedge:** One guided session, all supported sports from day one. Multi-sport is just prompt parameterization — no extra engineering. The session runner proves the hypothesis: "Will people complete these sessions repeatedly when the app runs the workout for them?" That's the only question that matters at launch. Sports in v1: running, climbing, cycling, lifting (exercise catalogue must cover all four).

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B — Train Stack Module, Native-first with AI.**

The core risk to eliminate is "will people use this on their phone before a run." Web doesn't answer that question. The Train Stack alignment makes native not just strategically correct but structurally necessary — the sport profile, session data, and pain history collected here are directly consumed by Train Stack's recommendation engine. Building on the wrong platform creates an expensive rewrite.

AI is the core, not a layer-on, because hardcoded protocols require someone to produce and maintain content at scale — AI scales it. The protocol generation can start simple (sport + 3 intake questions → Claude API → 4-5 exercise session) and improve as session completion and pain-before/after data accumulates.

**Key wireframe (4 screens):**

1. Sport onboarding — multi-sport selector. All sports active in v1 (running, climbing, cycling, lifting). No coming-soon gate — multi-sport is prompt parameterization, not extra engineering.
2. Home — today's session as primary CTA, daily pain check (= pain-before for today's session, not a separate concept), streak
3. Session runner — full-screen, sequential, autoplay images/video, 3 form cues, sets/reps/rest
4. Completion — minimal stats, pain-after rating

**Session generation policy:** Generated once per day at first open. Intake uses **capacity/trust scale (0-4 per body region)** — "how much do you trust this region today?" — not a pain scale. Readiness (Low/Normal/High) adjusts session intensity: Low = reduce sets by 1, prefer lower-impact variants; High = full protocol. Sport + capacity ratings + readiness is re-asked at each generation. The generated session is persisted to the `sessions` table with a `session_date` column; on app open, the client checks for a `sessions` row for today before calling the generation endpoint — no client-side cache. On AI generation failure: fall back to a hardcoded baseline protocol for the user's primary sport; surface a non-blocking retry banner. V1 supports all primary sports (running, climbing, cycling, lifting) — no coming-soon gate. Hardcoded fallback protocols needed for each supported sport.

**AI output schema** (Claude called with structured output / tool_use, validated server-side via Zod):

```typescript
interface GeneratedSession {
  title: string; // e.g. "Runner's Knee Prevention"
  estimated_duration_min: number; // e.g. 12; AI instructed to keep ≤ 15
  exercises: Array<{
    id: string;
    name: string;
    sets: number;
    reps: number | null; // null if duration-based
    duration_sec: number | null; // null if rep-based
    rest_sec: number;
    side: "bilateral" | "left_right";
    // bilateral = do both sides in one set (e.g. squats)
    // left_right = perform separately per side; runner auto-advances to
    //   mirrored repeat before moving to next exercise
    form_cues: string[]; // max 3
    video_url: string; // Self-hosted video URL (Mux/Cloudflare Stream) for form demonstration
    // Looked up server-side from KNOWN_EXERCISES catalogue — not generated by AI
    // fallback: show exercise name + form cues if video unavailable
  }>;
}
```

Server validates response against this Zod schema, retries once on malformed output, then falls back to hardcoded baseline. `packages/api` exports this type for use by native and web clients. AI system prompt instructs: max 5 exercises, total estimated duration ≤ 15 min, no exercises requiring equipment beyond bodyweight/resistance band, use only exercise names that match known `image_key` values.

**Example system prompt skeleton:**

```
You are a sports physiologist generating a prehab session.
Sport: {sport}. Readiness: {readiness} (Low = reduce sets by 1, prefer lower-impact; High = full protocol).
Training volume this week: {volume_hrs}.
Body region capacity ratings (0=no trust, 4=full trust): {capacity_ratings}.
Rules: max 5 exercises, ≤15 min total, bodyweight/band only.
Use only these exercise names (pre-filtered by sport relevance from shared catalogue): {KNOWN_EXERCISES}.
Return JSON matching this schema: {schema}.
```

**Paywall gate:** 3-week trial gives full access. After trial expiry, the session runner is fully gated. On app launch, always call RevenueCat `restorePurchases()` before showing the paywall; store `trial_started_at` server-side keyed to `user.id` as backstop against trial reset on reinstall. Paywall screen minimum content: trial expiry message, "$12/month" price, single "Subscribe" CTA, "Restore purchases" link (App Store 3.1.1 compliance), and "Cancel anytime — no hidden fees" trust badge.

**Notifications:** After each session completion, schedule a next-day reminder at user's preferred time. Expo Notifications. Required for the 40% repeat-session metric — not a future feature.

**Progressive session generation:** Next session is generated only after the previous is completed (not on a fixed calendar schedule). This makes the AI feel live rather than pre-packaged and creates a natural engagement loop. Users who complete consistently get progressively harder sessions; users who struggle get easier ones. Session regeneration with explicit feedback ("too easy / too hard / pain increased") feeds into the next generation prompt. On-demand session types (activation / mobility / recovery) available alongside the main program — follow Adapted's taxonomy.

**Progress measurement loop (High priority):** Measure → program → remeasure → show delta. At onboarding: capture baseline sport metric (e.g. "how far can you run before pain?" or "what grade are you climbing?"). After 4 weeks: prompt re-assessment. Surface the delta explicitly: "You went from 2K to 5K pain-free." This is the retention hook and the word-of-mouth driver. Build the data model for it from day one even if the UI is simple at launch.

**Build sequence** (AI schema first, then UX built against it):

1. Add `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY: z.string().min(1)` to `packages/env/src/server.ts` and `.env.example`. Add PostHog SDK to `apps/server` and Expo app — session recording + event tracking from day one (invaluable for early product decisions; Adapted uses it). Then: Claude API oRPC endpoint in `apps/server` — define `GeneratedSession` schema in `packages/api`, add exercise catalogue at `packages/api/src/constants/exercises.ts` — shared pool of ~100-200 exercises, each entry: `{ name: string, muscle_group: string, movement_pattern: string, sport_tags: string[], youtube_id: string }`. Exercises are not sport-owned — they are movement-pattern/muscle-group based and tagged with the sports they're most relevant to (e.g. a single-leg deadlift is tagged `['running', 'climbing', 'cycling']`). The server filters by user's `sport_tags` before injecting into the prompt. AI selects from the filtered subset — it never invents exercise names. Wire intake → Claude (tool_use / structured output, retry with "Your previous response was malformed JSON. Try again:" prefix + temperature 0.2 on retry) → validated JSON response, hardcoded fallback per sport
2. Drizzle schema:
   - `sport_profiles` (`user_id`, `primary_sport`, `sports[]`, `training_volume_hrs_per_week`, `capacity_ratings` JSONB — e.g. `{"knee": 2, "shoulder": 4, "back": 3}` using 0-4 scale)
   - `sessions` (`user_id`, `session_date` — with `UNIQUE(user_id, session_date)` + `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` to prevent race condition on concurrent opens, `exercises` JSONB column — see note below, `completed_at`, `drop_off_exercise_index`)
   - `capacity_logs` (`session_id` nullable, `user_id`, `capacity_ratings_before` JSONB, `capacity_ratings_after` JSONB, 0-4 scale per region; session_id nullable for orphan daily check-ins)
   - `progress_milestones` (`user_id`, `sport`, `milestone_type` e.g. "ran_5k_pain_free", `achieved_at`, `notes`) — for the progress measurement loop
   - `subscriptions` (`user_id`, `trial_started_at`, `subscribed_at`, `revenue_cat_customer_id`) — keep separate from Better-Auth's `user` table to avoid schema drift on auth updates
   - `notification_preferences` (`user_id`, `preferred_time` time-of-day, `timezone` IANA string, `enabled` boolean)
   - Streak = consecutive calendar days with at least one completed session; a rest day breaks it.
   - **Note on `session_exercises`:** Store exercises as JSONB on `sessions.exercises` for v1 (no cross-session analytics needed yet). Add `session_exercises` relational table only when exercise-level analytics become a stated requirement.
   - No Train Stack-specific fields in this iteration.
3. Build exercise catalogue: ~100-200 exercises total (not per sport). Each exercise is a movement pattern tagged with relevant sports — e.g. single-leg deadlift tagged `['running', 'climbing', 'cycling']`. For TestFlight: curate one YouTube video ID per exercise. Film self-hosted versions (Mux) in parallel. Populate `packages/api/src/constants/exercises.ts`. The AI selects from the sport-filtered subset of this shared pool — exercises belong to muscle groups and movement patterns, not sports. Add a video player component to the Expo app.
4. Expo sport profile onboarding (sport, training volume, readiness selector, pain areas)
5. Session runner UX — full-screen, sequential, images, rep/timer, form cues, completion logging
6. Wire native client to session generation endpoint via oRPC/TanStack Query; handle loading, error, and fallback states. TanStack Query `staleTime: 0` to force server check on each app open — no persistent local cache (no AsyncStorage). In-memory query cache is acceptable.
7. Home screen — today's session card (fetches or uses cached `sessions` row for today), streak, pain check
8. Notification scheduling on session completion
9. Hard paywall (RevenueCat) — 3-week trial, $12/mo ($120/yr), `restorePurchases()` on launch. Pre-submission checklist: App Store guideline 5.1.1 (privacy nutrition label for health data), 5.2 (clinical authority disclaimer inside the app), 3.1.1/3.1.2 (paywall screen with Restore Purchases). Submit to TestFlight external testing before paywall goes live to surface rejection reasons early. 15% rate assumes Small Business Program eligibility — validate before financial planning.

## Success Criteria

- **Session completion rate ≥ 60%** at 7 days (will people actually finish the guided session?)
- **Repeat session rate ≥ 40%** at 14 days (will people come back without prompting?)
- **Capacity rating improvement:** Users report equal or higher capacity ratings after 4 weeks of consistent use
- **Progress milestone:** At least one sport-specific progress milestone logged per active user within 30 days (e.g. "ran 5K pain-free")
- **Conversion:** 3-week trial → paid ≥ 10% (RevenueCat 2026 benchmark for hard paywall)
- **$200K profit by end of year one** (~1,500 subscribers at $15/mo or ~1,850 at $12/mo = ~$270K revenue; assumes ~$70K costs: Claude API, RevenueCat, hosting, Apple 15% cut)
