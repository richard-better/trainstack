## Vision

### 10x Check

The 10x version of Pre is Pre becoming the prehab module of Train Stack. Pre's job is to collect the ground truth: which sports you train, how your body responds, which body regions you trust, which sessions you complete. That data — sport profile, capacity ratings, session history, injury patterns — is exactly what Train Stack's "What should I train today?" engine needs to make intelligent recommendations across your full training life. Pre isn't trying to grow into a sport continuity engine independently. It IS the continuity engine's foundation. The 10x outcome is a user who starts on Pre for their knee, builds the habit, and one day opens Train Stack to find it already knows their body — because Pre told it everything.

### Competitive Context

The design doc's competitive analysis is comprehensive but missing one critical development: Strava acquired Recover Athletics, a runner-specific prehab app. With 120M+ Strava users, this creates a bundling threat if Strava integrates runner prehab into their subscription. The strategic response is explicit: go multi-sport faster, go AI-deeper, and go standalone. A Strava-locked prehab feature cannot serve climbers, cyclists, and lifters with the same fidelity Pre can — that's the moat.

### Session model — scaffold + lazy generation (KEY DECISION)

The session model is NOT "generate once per day at first open." It is NOT "generate only after previous completion." It is a hybrid:

1. **Program scaffold:** At onboarding (and weekly thereafter), the AI generates a sequence of session themes/types for upcoming days (e.g., Day 1: full runner prehab, Day 2: hip stability focus, Day 3: ankle + calf). This is stored in `program_schedule` table — lightweight, just session type + theme per day.
2. **Lazy full generation:** On the day of, when the user opens the app, the server generates the full exercise list for today's scaffolded session. It incorporates: (a) yesterday's session completion data, (b) today's readiness check (1 question: Low/Normal/High), (c) any on-demand override ("give me a quick running prehab instead").
3. **Daily intake = 1-2 taps:** Readiness + inference from session history. NOT 6 body-region capacity ratings every day (those are onboarding + weekly re-assessment only).
4. **Streaks = calendar-based:** Consecutive days with a completed session.
5. **UNIQUE(user_id, session_date)** schema is correct — full generated sessions are keyed by date.

## Accessibility & Responsive Requirements (from /plan-design-review)

**Expo app (all screens):**

- Minimum touch target: 44px × 44px for all tappable elements (buttons, chips, capacity dots, skip links)
- Capacity dots: accessible label reads "[Region] trust: [N] out of 4" (e.g. "Knee trust: 2 out of 4")
- VoiceOver/TalkBack labels required on all interactive components (sport chips, readiness chips, action buttons, progress bar)
- Color contrast: DESIGN.md warm white (`#F0EDE8`) on dark background (`#0C0C0F`) = ~14:1 ratio — compliant. Muted text (`#7A7773`) on dark bg = ~4.6:1 — passes AA. Verify terracotta (`#D4603A`) on dark bg for any text use.
- Progress bar: `accessibilityRole="progressbar"` with `accessibilityValue={{ min: 0, max: 5, now: currentIndex }}`

**Waitlist page (Astro):**

- Semantic HTML: `<header>`, `<main>`, `<form>`, `<footer>` landmarks
- Email input: `<label>` associated via `for`/`id`, `aria-required="true"`, `aria-describedby` pointing to "Launching on iOS" note
- CTA button: `type="submit"`, explicit text label (no icon-only)
- Illustration: `alt` text describing sport subject (e.g. "Classical illustration of a runner in terracotta and parchment tones")
- Mobile: single-column below 768px, illustration below fold — no content loss
